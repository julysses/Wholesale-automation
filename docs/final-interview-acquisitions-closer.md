## Purpose

This final interview confirms three things that the role-play and screen cannot fully reveal:

1. **Accountability mindset** — can the candidate own a number, own a miss, and course-correct without excuses?
2. **Culture fit** — do they align with our "AI-first, human-close" operating model and fast-paced environment?
3. **Compensation and runway alignment** — are expectations realistic for our commission-heavy structure?

---

## Interview Structure (45 min)

| Block | Time | Owner | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-up / context setting | 5 min | CMO | Explain the company, AI dialer model, and team structure |
| Behavioral questions | 20 min | CMO + CEO | Accountability, urgency, coachability |
| Deal accountability deep-dive | 10 min | CEO | Forecast discipline and loss ownership |
| Compensation alignment | 5 min | CMO | Set expectations, confirm interest |
| Candidate Q&A | 5 min | Both | Assess what they care about |

## Section 1: Warm-Up / Context Setting (CMO-Led, 5 min)

Cover:
- Our AI dialer handles cold outreach. Their job starts when a lead is marked HOT.
- Speed-to-contact (≤60 min) is the single most important metric.
- Commission-first structure: base covers expenses, commission is the income engine.
- Close 5+ deals/month to be considered high performer in our model.

*Gauge their reaction — do they lean in or show hesitation?*

---

## Section 2: Behavioral Questions (20 min)

Ask 3–4 questions. Take notes on specifics — vague answers are a yellow flag.

### Q1 — Urgency Under Pressure
> "Tell me about a time you had a hot lead that needed a response within the hour, but you were in the middle of something else. What did you do?"

**Look for**: Self-interruption, prioritization instinct, outcome focus. Red flag: describes asking a manager what to do.

---

### Q2 — Accountability on a Miss
> "Walk me through a deal you lost that you felt you should have closed. What happened, and what did you change?"

**Look for**: Specific deal, direct ownership without blaming the seller or the market, behavioral change that followed. Red flag: "The seller wasn't serious" with no self-reflection.

---

### Q3 — Handling a Seller Who Goes Cold
> "You follow up on a HOT lead within 45 minutes. The seller answers but says they've changed their mind and need to think about it. What do you do in the next 24 hours?"

**Look for**: Re-engagement strategy (specific callback times, adding value, not just waiting), persistence without harassment. Red flag: "I'd give them space and try again next week."

---

### Q4 — Coachability (Use Only If Time)
> "Describe a time your manager gave you feedback on how you were handling negotiations. How did you respond and what changed?"

**Look for**: Non-defensive reception, specific adjustment made, measurable impact. Red flag: Reframes feedback as wrong or situational.

---

## Section 3: Deal Accountability Deep-Dive (CEO-Led, 10 min)

This section assesses forecast discipline — the candidate's ability to own a pipeline number.

### Q5 — Pipeline Forecast
> "If you had 12 active leads in your pipeline right now, how would you determine which ones to prioritize and what your close forecast would be for the next 30 days?"

**Look for**: A structured approach (e.g., by motivation score, timeline, ask vs. MAO gap). Should produce a number, not a range with heavy caveats. Red flag: "It really depends" with no framework given.

---

### Q6 — MAO Discipline
> "You're in a negotiation. The seller won't move below $205,000 and our MAO is $195,000. What do you do?"

**Look for**: Escalation path — they should know to pause the negotiation and escalate, not override the MAO unilaterally. Correct answer: "I'd hold at $195k, explain I need to check if there's any flexibility, and loop in my manager before countering above MAO."

**Auto-fail signal**: "I'd go to $205k to save the deal and sort it out later."

---

## Section 4: Compensation Alignment (CMO-Led, 5 min)

Cover structure clearly:
- **Base**: $2,500–$3,000/month (covers expenses, not income)
- **Commission**: $1,500–$3,000 per closed deal (tiered on volume)
- **At-target earnings (5 deals/month)**: $10,500–$18,000/month
- **Ramp**: 30-day grace period with training deals supported by manager

Ask: *"Based on this structure, does your 30-day close target feel achievable given what you've been doing?"*

**Look for**: Confidence backed by math ("I've been closing 6-7 deals/month, so yes"). Red flag: Excitement about the ceiling with no mention of the floor.

---

## Section 5: Candidate Q&A (5 min)

Note what they ask — questions reveal priorities.

**Strong signals**: Questions about the AI handoff process, CRM workflow, deal criteria/MAO guardrails, team structure.
**Neutral**: Questions about culture, growth path.
**Weak signals**: No questions, or only questions about comp details already covered.

## Scoring Rubric

Score each dimension 1–5. Minimum to advance: **22/30 total, no dimension below 3**.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Urgency / Speed instinct** | Passive, waits for direction | Responds but not self-directed | Proactively re-prioritizes in real-time | /5 |
| **Accountability** | Externalizes blame | Acknowledges miss, vague lesson | Owns it fully, specific behavioral change | /5 |
| **MAO Discipline** | Would exceed without escalation | Hesitates but would escalate | Immediate escalation, firm on guardrails | /5 |
| **Pipeline / Forecast thinking** | No framework | Has a process, imprecise | Structured, quantified, confident | /5 |
| **Culture / AI-first mindset** | Skeptical or passive about tech | Neutral, okay with the model | Energized by warm-lead model, asks good questions | /5 |
| **Compensation realism** | Only focused on ceiling | Understands structure, some concern | Backs target with math from past performance | /5 |

**Total: /30**
